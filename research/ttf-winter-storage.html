EU gas storage has moved into withdrawal early: long TTF into winter

23 October 2026. Kyle Glatley. TTF front-month, ICE Endex.

EU gas storage moved into net withdrawal on 6 October, roughly three weeks earlier than the 2021 to 2025 median. If withdrawals continue at around 1.8 bcm per week, storage will fall below the seasonal average by mid-December, leaving Europe a thinner buffer for the January and February demand peak than current prices imply.

TTF front-month daily close, EUR/MWh, 29 Apr to 29 Oct 2026. Source: LSEG Datastream.

Drivers of the rally

TTF front-month rose from 40.31 EUR/MWh in late June to 99.60 EUR/MWh in late October. The seasonal recovery in European gas prices usually takes place gradually through the autumn; this year most of it was concentrated in a single quarter. The move has been driven primarily by supply. Norwegian pipeline maintenance in September and October reduced exports to mainland Europe by roughly 8% relative to 2025, while Asian buyers, mainly Japanese and Korean utilities rebuilding their own inventories, have been outbidding European importers for uncommitted LNG cargoes.

Demand has added to the pressure. Cold weather arrived in central Europe around two weeks ahead of the ten-year average, bringing heating demand forward.

The storage trajectory

The central argument concerns storage. EU storage entered October at approximately 92% of capacity, against a five-year average of 95% for the same date. The level alone does not suggest a shortage. The more important indicator is the rate of change: net injections turned negative on 6 October, around three weeks earlier than the 2021 to 2025 median, and storage is now falling by approximately 1.8 bcm per week.

If withdrawals continue at this pace through November, which current temperature forecasts suggest is likely, storage will fall below the seasonal norm by mid-December. Europe would then enter January and February, the period of highest demand, with less gas in storage than at the same point in 2021 and 2023.

Entry and stop

The position was opened at 93.25 on 23 October, following two consecutive closes above the September high of 83.33. Since entry, the price has fallen to around 90.00 intraday on three occasions and recovered each time. This is consistent with utilities and industrial consumers securing winter supply rather than short-term speculative positioning.

The stop is set at 80.00, below the former September high, which should now act as support. A close below 80.00 would indicate either a significant fall in demand or additional supply not accounted for at entry; in either case the basis for the trade would no longer hold.

Target and risks

The target of 127.00 gives a reward-to-risk ratio of approximately 2.55 to 1 (33.75 of potential gain against 13.25 of potential loss). In 2022 and 2023 this was roughly the level at which European industry began to reduce gas consumption materially, which has historically limited further price increases.

The main risk is a mild November, which would slow withdrawals and allow the market to reduce the scarcity premium. A second risk is US LNG export utilisation rising above 90% of capacity, which would increase supply to the Atlantic basin. The renewal of the Ukrainian transit contract, due in January 2027, could move prices in either direction.

The position has a notional value of EUR 100,000. A close at the stop would result in a loss of approximately EUR 14,200.
